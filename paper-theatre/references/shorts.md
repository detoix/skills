# Requested vertical shorts

## Selection and layout

- Treat a short as its own edit and 9:16 stage, normally 1080×1920 at the film's frame rate. Choose a self-contained question, explanation and payoff; preserve the source's qualifications when shortening it.
- Recompose figures, maps, labels, title and stage decoration for portrait viewing. Do not treat a horizontal frame placed inside a vertical border as the completed adaptation.
- Establish the subject or hook from the opening frame. Use readable burned-in captions and leave room for the destination's interface; check the current target layout rather than treating one fixed safe-area percentage as universal.
- Carry the film's approved material treatment, typography, palette and musical character into the new composition. End with the agreed next action or related-film reference only where it fits the requested publication plan.

## Audio identity and timing

- Reuse selected recordings from the current approved film when the requested short can be made from them. Identify lines by stable ID and check selected-file hashes against `audio/manifest.json`; stop reuse when the source and manifest disagree.
- Preserve the recorded source and its provenance. Any separately requested new narration uses the normal voice workflow and gets its own provenance.
- Build a new timeline for the short, accounting for source trim intervals, chosen line order and new gaps. Recalculate every caption, visual anchor and sound cue on that timeline.
- Inspect words following pauses against actual speech onsets. Correct alignments that span silence, preserve word order and check the correction in context before deriving word-by-word captions or animation.
- Keep image and effects on the same anchors. Apply the same separate-bed/foreground music rules as the full film and check the opening, all musical breaks, speech returns and ending.

## Verification

- Check portrait dimensions, frame rate, stream duration, full decoding and loudness/true peak in the final encoded file.
- Inspect frames extracted from the finished short at the opening, crowded moments, caption changes, maps and ending. Check legibility and interface clearance at phone size, including the lowest and rightmost text.
- Record which full-film version, timeline and selected audio hashes the short derives from. When a source correction changes an included fact, recording or title, review the affected short and its publication text together.
